How Can We Gather Preliminary Information?

Use several sources. A manager’s explanation is one perspective to check.
PEOPLE

Managers & employees

Informal / unstructured interviews: hear different perspectives.

RECORDS

Performance reports, HR records and exit information.

LITERATURE

Keep participation voluntary and responses confidential.
Preliminary investigation clarifies the problem; it does not replace the full study.

FIND THE PROBLEM

Try It: What Is the Real Problem?

Possible explanations are questions to investigate, not findings already established.
LOW SYSTEM ADOPTION
Six months after launch, employee usage of a new digital system remains low.

Management says: “Employees resist technology.”

DISCUSS BEFORE REVEALING
1   What is the observation?
2   What is the assumption?
3   What would you investigate?
Observation: Low usage.   Assumption: Resistance to technology.
Investigate: Training, ease of use, system quality, usefulness, management support and workload.

FRAME THE RESEARCH

What Can the Research Gap Look Like?

A gap does not have to mean that nobody has ever studied the topic.
KNOWLEDGE

Not adequately explained

A relationship is less understood, or findings are inconclusive or inconsistent.

METHODOLOGICAL

Methods leave an issue unresolved

A previous design, measure or analysis limits what can be concluded.

CONTEXTUAL

A relevant setting needs attention

Explain why existing findings may not apply to this population or context.

Be humble: “No study has examined…” requires evidence. A new location alone does not establish a meaningful gap.

What Makes a Good Research Problem?

A good problem statement is focused and specific; the problem must also pass these three checks.
1

RELEVANT

Does it matter?

Addresses a real managerial issue or a defensible academic need.

2

FEASIBLE

Can we investigate it?

Fits our skills, access, time and available resources.

3

INTERESTING

Will it sustain our interest?

Worth investigating and motivating enough to complete.

ALIGNMENT MATTERS

Problem Statement → Research Question

Move from a focused problem to a question the study can answer.
PROBLEM / RESEARCH NEED
Employee motivation appears low. The role of employee reward needs examination.
What do we need to know?
RESEARCH QUESTION

Does employee reward influence employee motivation?

Translates the problem into a specific need for information.

Keep the same concepts and research intention as the problem. A question is not an answer already assumed.
The wording guides the study; evidence and an appropriate design are needed to answer it.

Research Question ↔ Research Objective

The two should describe the same research intention from different angles.
RESEARCH QUESTION · WHAT?

What do we want to learn?

Translates the problem into a specific need for information.
RQ: Does employee reward influence employee motivation?
↔

SAME
INTENTION

RESEARCH OBJECTIVE · PURPOSE

What will the study examine?

States what the research intends to accomplish.
RO: To examine the effect of employee reward on employee motivation.
If the RQ changes, the RO should change with it. Alignment keeps the research focused.

Set the Boundaries: Research Scope

What does this study cover — and what does it not cover? An illustrative turnover-study scope.
TOPIC / PURPOSEWorkload, supervisor support and intention to leave.
POPULATION / SAMPLEEmployees in the selected organisation; specify who is included.
GEOGRAPHICAL SETTINGThe organisation’s Kedah branches.
TIME PERIODInformation collected during a defined three-month period.
CONCEPTUAL BOUNDARIESFocus on the selected concepts. Intention to leave is not the same as actual turnover; salary and other factors are outside this illustrative study’s focus.
Scope sets the boundaries. Findings should be interpreted within those boundaries.

FRAME THE RESEARCH

Why Does This Study Matter?

Scope asks what is covered. Significance explains why the study is needed.
KNOWLEDGE / ACADEMIC VALUE

What could it add to understanding?

Address a supported knowledge gap.
Clarify findings in the selected context.

Turnover focus: Improve understanding of workload, supervisor support and intention to leave.

PRACTICAL VALUE

Who could benefit — and how?

Help HR managers examine retention concerns.
Inform workload and supervisor-support decisions.

Claim realistic potential benefits. Findings do not guarantee that turnover will fall.

Significance follows from the research problem; it need not benefit every possible group.
