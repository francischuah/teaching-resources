CHAPTER 8 · SLIDE 20

Adopt, adapt or develop?

Adopt

Use an existing instrument without substantive modification. Check suitability and permissions.

Adapt

Modify for context, language or population. Reassess measurement quality.

Develop

Create a new instrument. Requires substantial development and validation.

Look for suitable established instruments first; cite the source.

CHAPTER 8 · SLIDE 21

Five steps in questionnaire development

1. Content — Information and constructs needed
2. Response format — Open or closed questions; response scale
3. Wording — Clear, specific and unbiased
4. Sequence — Logical order; sensitive questions near the end
5. Cover letter — Purpose, voluntary participation, confidentiality, researcher details and thanks

CHAPTER 8 · SLIDE 26

Validity

Content validity

Do items adequately cover the construct?

Construct validity

Does evidence support the intended interpretation?

Criterion validity

Does measurement relate appropriately to an external criterion?

Example: punctuality alone may not adequately cover job performance.

CHAPTER 8 · SLIDE 27

Reliable but not valid?

Consistency does not guarantee appropriate measurement. Target analogy only, not a formal validity test.

CHAPTER 8 · SLIDE 28

Evaluate the measurement instrument

A researcher measures job satisfaction with:

Item 1

I arrive at work on time.

Item 2

I complete assigned tasks.

Item 3

I meet performance targets.

Cronbach’s alpha = 0.91 (illustrative). Is this a good satisfaction instrument?

No. These items concern work behaviour or performance. High alpha does not establish validity.
