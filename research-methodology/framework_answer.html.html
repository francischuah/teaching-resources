Use this guide after completing your own attempt

Find your scenario below. Compare your variable roles and diagram first, then reveal the model H₀/H₁ pairs. These are proposed research hypotheses, not findings from analysed data.

Direct path

One solid arrow links a predictor to an outcome. Write a pair for each proposed arrow, including the two component paths involving a mediator.

Mediation

Explain how X reaches Y through M. Add an indirect-effect hypothesis for the complete X → M → Y pathway.

Moderation

Explain when or for whom an effect is stronger or weaker. Name the moderator, the exact relationship and the expected change.

About the wording: Here, H₀ states a zero effect; H₁ adds the direction proposed in the scenario. Equivalent wording using “no significant effect” and “a significant positive/negative effect” is commonly used in class. A formal one-sided test needs its directional null and decision rule specified separately; these model answers do not prescribe a statistical testing procedure.

For mediation, the indirect effect is the target; the two component hypotheses do not replace it. For moderation, hypothesise a change in an effect, rather than merely a direct effect of the moderator on the outcome. Do not conclude “full” or “partial” mediation from a diagram alone.

Revision · What must you know?

  • Identify IVs, the DV, mediators and moderators from a business scenario.
  • Match each hypothesis to a path or mechanism in your diagram.
  • Distinguish a direct effect from an indirect effect through a mediator.
  • State which relationship is moderated and how its strength changes.
  • Use the direction given by the scenario: positive, negative, strengthened or buffered.

Key takeaways

A good framework, clear variable roles and well-written hypotheses must describe the same proposed relationships. Your labels and wording can differ from this guide if their meanings remain accurate and consistent with the scenario.

These are sample answers for the ten supplied scenarios. They are not automatic grading rules or evidence that a proposed relationship is statistically supported.
