Four Levels of Measurement

The same number can represent a category code, a rank or a quantity. Classify by meaning, not appearance.

LevelPropertiesExample
NominalCategory onlyDepartment code
OrdinalCategory + orderEducation level
IntervalOrder + equal intervals; no absolute zeroTemperature in °C
RatioEqual intervals + true zeroMonthly income

Decision tree

Meaningful order? No → Nominal; Yes ↓
↓
Equal intervals? No → Ordinal; Yes ↓
↓
Meaningful absolute zero? No → Interval; Yes → Ratio
An individual Likert-type item is ordinal. Multi-item composite scores are commonly treated as approximately interval when justified.

11

Reliability

Reliability concerns consistency of measurement.

Stability

Test–retest: repeat the same measure.
Parallel forms: compare equivalent measures.

Internal consistency

Inter-item consistency: Cronbach’s alpha.
Split-half: compare two halves of an instrument.

Cronbach’s alpha is a commonly used indicator of internal consistency, not a test of validity. SPSS procedures are covered in Chapter 10.

12

Validity

Validity concerns whether evidence supports the intended interpretation of a measurement.
TypeQuestion
Content validityDo items adequately cover the construct?
Construct validityDoes evidence support the intended interpretation?
Criterion validityDoes measurement relate appropriately to an external criterion?

For example, punctuality alone may not adequately cover job performance.

Target analogy only, not a formal validity test.

High reliability does not establish validity.
A job satisfaction instrument has alpha = 0.91, but items measure punctuality, task completion and performance targets. Is it appropriate?

No. The items appear to measure work behaviour or performance, rather than job satisfaction.

Apply Your Understanding

Attempt each question before revealing the suggested answer. Identify → Explain → Apply → Justify.

Part A — Conceptual Understanding

1. Distinguish conceptual and operational definitions using employee motivation.
Reveal suggested answer

Conceptual: psychological drive to exert effort towards work goals. Operational: specify a multi-item questionnaire measuring effort, persistence and drive on a seven-point agreement scale.

2. Identify and justify the measurement level: employee ID, low/medium/high satisfaction, Celsius temperature, monthly expenditure.
Reveal suggested answer

Employee ID: nominal (category). Satisfaction categories: ordinal (order, unknown intervals). Celsius: interval (equal intervals, no absolute zero). Expenditure: ratio (equal intervals and meaningful zero).

3. Explain reliability and validity. Can an instrument be reliable but not valid? Give an example.
Reveal suggested answer

Reliability is consistency; validity concerns evidence for intended interpretation. A scale consistently adding 5 kg is consistent but inaccurate. A questionnaire may consistently measure performance rather than intended satisfaction.

Part B — Application and Critical Thinking

4. A study examines trust in AI and intention to use AI. Propose three trust items and a response format.
Reveal suggested answer

Illustrative items: I believe AI provides dependable information; I feel confident relying on AI; I believe AI performs tasks reliably. Use a seven-point agreement response format. Prefer a suitable established instrument.

5. “I am satisfied with my salary and working conditions.” Identify the problem, explain the consequence and improve it.
Reveal suggested answer

Double-barrelled item: a respondent may be satisfied with salary but dissatisfied with conditions. Separate into two statements, one about salary and one about working conditions.

6. Job satisfaction is measured using punctuality, task completion and performance targets. Alpha = 0.91. Evaluate the instrument.
Reveal suggested answer

High alpha suggests internal consistency, but the items appear to measure work behaviour or performance rather than job satisfaction. Validity is not established; reconsider the definition and items.
