Theoretical Framework & Hypothesis Development

A research problem tells us what needs investigation. The literature helps us understand what is known. A theoretical framework now brings the relevant variables and explanations together, leading to hypotheses we can investigate.

1

A framework needs relationships and reasons

A theoretical framework identifies the relevant variables, shows how they are proposed to relate and explains why those relationships are expected.

The model

Supervisor support → Turnover intention.

The explanation

Support may help employees feel valued and reduce their intention to leave.

Our continuing example: We observed an increase in employee turnover in Chapter 3 and evaluated relevant evidence in Chapter 4. Now we propose relationships to investigate.
Actual turnover: leaving the organisation.
Turnover intention: thinking about or planning to leave.
Identify → Connect → Explain. Define the variables, specify the relationships, and justify them using theory and relevant literature.

All relationships on this page are teaching proposals unless stated otherwise. They are not presented as empirical findings.

2

Concepts, constructs and variables

Concept

A general idea used to describe something—for example, support.

Construct

An abstract concept deliberately defined for research—for example, perceived supervisor support.

Variable

A characteristic that takes different values across people or situations. It may be observed or latent.

Latent means not directly observed. Responses to relevant questionnaire items can serve as observed indicators of an abstract construct.

These terms can overlap. Define clearly how you use them in your study; not every variable is directly observable.

Sample items retained or lightly edited from the supplied teaching deck. They do not by themselves constitute a validated scale.

Think: is the item the same as the construct?

No. The construct is the defined abstract idea; an item provides an observed response used to represent it. The measure must fit the intended meaning.

The diagram is not the whole framework. A theory—such as the Theory of Planned Behaviour example in the presentation—provides reasoning about how and why constructs relate.

“Support reduces intention to leave because it improves job satisfaction.” What role does satisfaction play?

Answer and explanation

Mediator. The explanation proposes satisfaction as the mechanism. In contrast, a statement that the support–intention relationship differs by job opportunities assigns opportunities a moderator role.

A variable’s role depends on the proposed explanation. Define the role before drawing the arrows.
7

Develop clear, testable hypotheses

A hypothesis is a proposition that can be examined empirically. It should fit the research purpose, state a clear relationship and have theoretical and literature support.

H₀ · Null hypothesis

There is no relationship between supervisor support and turnover intention.

H₁ · Alternative hypothesis

There is a relationship between supervisor support and turnover intention.

These are introductory substantive statements. A statistical test requires a precise parameter-based formulation.

Directional versus non-directional

“If–then” is another wording format. For example: if supervisor support increases, turnover intention is expected to decrease. The relationship still needs justification.

Non-directional

Supervisor support is related to turnover intention.

Negative direction

Higher support is associated with lower turnover intention.

Positive direction

Higher support is associated with higher job satisfaction.

Relationship → Reason → Evidence. Explain the expected relationship, give its theoretical reasoning and use relevant literature to justify it.
An arrow does not prove causation. Directional wording alone does not decide the full statistical testing procedure.
8

Practise writing a hypothesis

Choose a relationship from the model and write a matching hypothesis. You can use non-directional wording unless you can justify a direction.

Practice only. Your writing is not saved when you close or reload this page.

Compare with possible statements
  • Likeability is related to customer loyalty.
  • Job satisfaction mediates the relationship between supervisor support and turnover intention.
  • Alternative job opportunities moderate the relationship between supervisor support and turnover intention.

A directional moderation hypothesis must specify and justify how the relationship changes.

Check: same variables, same relationship, clear wording, justified prediction.

Revision: What Must You Know?

Select an answer, then check your understanding. Each explanation also works without JavaScript.

1. What does a theoretical framework need?

Answer and explanation

Proposed relationships and an explanation of why they are expected. A framework connects the variables, relationships and supporting reasoning.

2. What is the DV in our continuing example?

Answer and explanation

Turnover intention. Turnover intention is the outcome we want to understand.

3. Job satisfaction explains how support may relate to intention. What is its role?

Answer and explanation

Mediator. A mediator proposes a mechanism.

4. Job opportunities change the support–intention relationship. What is the role?

Answer and explanation

Moderator. A moderator specifies a condition under which a relationship changes.

5. Which hypothesis is directional?

Answer and explanation

Higher support is associated with lower turnover intention. The second statement specifies a negative direction.

6. What does an observed association establish by itself?

Answer and explanation

A relationship, without establishing causation. Causal conclusions need more than the presence of an association.
