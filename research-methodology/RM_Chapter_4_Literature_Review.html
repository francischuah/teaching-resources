Evaluating & Documenting the Literature

A literature review helps you understand what is known and what still needs investigation. This learning page focuses on choosing useful evidence, comparing findings and writing a connected review that supports your research problem.

Your starting point: Turnover increased in an organisation. You now want to examine the relationship between supervisor support and turnover intention.
Actual turnover means leaving; turnover intention means thinking about or planning to leave. Keep the concepts distinct.
1

Screen first, then read closely

Screening helps you decide which sources deserve a closer look. It is the beginning of evaluation.

Title & abstract

Check the topic, purpose, population and main findings.

Book contents & chapter

Locate the part relevant to your research question.

Relevant full text

Examine the evidence, methods and limitations.

Citation counts are clues, not verdicts. A highly cited study still needs evaluation. Newer work may have fewer citations. Read how other researchers use or challenge it.
2

Evaluate a study with five questions

Use these questions while reading. Tick each one after you have considered it—not simply because the article looks convincing.

My reading checklist

0 of 5 questions considered.

A different country or a small sample is not automatically a weakness. Explain how the setting, sampling or method affects what you can conclude and how useful the evidence is for your study.
3

Try it: which evidence belongs in the review?

Fictional practice studies · Never use these as assignment references.

Study A · Service employees

One-time survey of 300 employees. Higher supervisor support was associated with lower turnover intention.

Study C · Hotel employees

One-time survey of 90 employees. No clear association was found.

Which would you include?

Explanation

Include both if relevant. Selecting only findings that support your expectation gives an incomplete picture. A difference needs investigation; the summaries alone cannot explain it. Neither survey proves that support causes employees to stay.

4

A literature matrix makes studies easier to compare. Record the author, setting and sample, method, relationship examined and findings. Add a note about how you will use the source.

Read across the matrix

Agreement

A and B suggest a similar association.

Difference

C reports no clear association.

Shared limitation

The matrix is a thinking tool. Your written review should explain the patterns and their relevance to the research problem.
5

Turn separate summaries into synthesis

Summary reports what a source says. Synthesis connects studies to explain an idea, pattern or unresolved issue.

SEPARATE SUMMARIES

Study A found an association between supervisor support and turnover intention. Study B found a similar association. Study C found no clear association.

The reader must make the comparison.

CONNECTED SYNTHESIS

Evidence from service and retail samples suggests that greater supervisor support is associated with lower turnover intention (Studies A and B). However, Study C found no clear association among hotel employees. Findings differ across these samples, but the summaries alone do not explain why.

The writer compares and qualifies the evidence.

Fictional study labels illustrate the writing logic; they are not valid citations.

A useful paragraph sequence:
Claim → Evidence → Comparison → Implication for your research

Try a synthesis sentence

How could you acknowledge the different findings without claiming to know why they differ?

Example sentence

Although Studies A and B suggest a relationship, Study C found no clear association; further examination of their settings, measures and methods is needed to interpret this difference.

Do not turn limited reading into “Nobody has studied this.” Check the wider literature before identifying a gap. A new setting alone does not automatically justify a study.
6

Document the review and acknowledge sources

A written literature review introduces the subject, highlights the problem and explains the relevant work so far.

Organise by ideas

For this topic: turnover intention → supervisor support → evidence on the relationship → unresolved issue and proposed study.

Keep sources traceable

Use the course’s APA 7 guidance. Match in-text citations with reference-list entries and check the original source details.

Paraphrase with understanding

  1. Read until you understand the idea.
  2. Put the source aside and explain the meaning in your own words.
  3. Compare your wording with the original for accuracy.
  4. Acknowledge the source of the idea.
Changing a few words is not enough. A paraphrased idea still needs a citation. An exact quotation must be clearly identified as a quotation and documented appropriately.
7

Write one connected paragraph

Use the fictional A–B–C matrix. State the shared finding, acknowledge the difference and a limitation, then identify what needs closer investigation.

Practice space only. Your writing is not saved when you close or reload this page.

Compare with a possible answer

Studies A and B associate greater supervisor support with lower turnover intention, whereas Study C reports no clear association. These one-time surveys do not demonstrate causation. Further reading is needed to establish whether the differences reflect context, measurement or other explanations.

Before finishing: Have you compared findings, preserved uncertainty and linked the evidence to your research problem?

Revision: What Must You Know?

Choose an answer for each question, then check your understanding. Explanations are also available without JavaScript.

1. Does a high citation count prove that a study is suitable?

Answer and explanation

No, relevance and evidence still need evaluation. Citation counts indicate attention, not automatic quality or relevance.

2. A relevant study contradicts your expectation. What should you do?

Answer and explanation

Evaluate and compare it with the other evidence. A review should preserve relevant differences rather than select only supportive findings.

3. What does synthesis do?

Answer and explanation

Connects studies to explain patterns and unresolved issues. Synthesis compares and interprets evidence around an idea.

4. Can a one-time survey establish that supervisor support causes employees to stay?

Answer and explanation

No. An association alone does not establish causation. A large sample does not by itself establish cause and effect.

5. Does a paraphrased idea need a citation?

Answer and explanation

Yes, the idea still comes from a source. Your own wording does not remove the need to acknowledge the source of an idea.
