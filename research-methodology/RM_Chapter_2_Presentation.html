Scientific Investigation

What makes research scientific, and how do researchers systematically move from an idea to evidence and a conclusion?

CHAPTER QUESTION · What makes an investigation credible enough for us to trust its conclusions?

What Is Scientific Research?

Scientific research is a step-by-step, logical, organised and rigorous way to identify problems, gather and analyse data, and draw valid conclusions.

LOGICAL

Reasoning connects the problem, evidence and conclusion.

→

ORGANISED

The investigation follows a planned structure.

→

SYSTEMATIC

Evidence is gathered and analysed using an orderly process.

→

RIGOROUS

Methods and conclusions are carefully justified.

Hunches, experience and intuition may contribute to managerial judgement—but scientific investigation provides a stronger evidence base.

Why Does Scientific Investigation Matter?

Systematic Evidence

Gather relevant information carefully.

→

More Objective Understanding

Focus attention on evidence rather than preference alone.

→

Accuracy & Confidence

State findings with greater confidence.

→

Better-Supported Decisions

Help managers identify and address critical problems.

Business connection: Scientific investigation and managerial decision-making work together in effective problem-solving.

Scientific Research ≠ Perfect Research

Think: Management research studies people, organisations, attitudes and perceptions. Can its findings ever be completely exact and error-free?

Subjective Concepts

Feelings, emotions, attitudes and perceptions are difficult to measure perfectly.

Representative Samples

It may be difficult to obtain a sample that perfectly represents the population.

Generalizability

Findings from one setting may not apply equally well to every other setting.

Why bother? Research need not be perfect to be useful. A disciplined investigation can still provide better evidence than relying only on assumptions or hunches.

The Hallmarks of Scientific Research

WHAT SHOULD GOOD SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH LOOK LIKE?
1

Purposiveness

Research is focused on a clearly identified and defined problem.

2

Rigor

A solid theoretical base and sound methodological design support the study.

3

Testability

The hypotheses or ideas can be examined using evidence.

4

Replicability

Similar research can be repeated and the findings supported again.

5

Precision & Confidence

Findings are estimated with an understood degree of accuracy and confidence.

6

Objectivity

Conclusions follow the analysed evidence rather than personal preference.

7

Generalizability

Findings may apply from one setting to others when appropriate.

8

Parsimony

Use an efficient explanation rather than unnecessary complexity.

These hallmarks work together. “Scientific” means the investigation is disciplined and defensible—not that it is perfect.

Can You Spot the Hallmark?

The study begins with a clearly defined organisational problem.

Purposiveness

Another researcher repeats a similar study and obtains comparable findings.

Replicability

The conclusion follows the analysed data rather than the manager's preference.

Objectivity

A simpler explanation accounts for the issue without unnecessary variables.

Parsimony

Basic Research vs Applied Research

BASIC RESEARCH

Main purpose: discover or expand knowledge.

Interest: often academic or theory-oriented.

Scope: knowledge may be relevant to a wider setting.

Timeline: often determined by the researcher.

APPLIED RESEARCH

Main purpose: solve or understand a problem faced by an organisation.

Interest: practitioners and organisations, as well as researchers.

Scope: solution may be localised to the problem at hand.

Timeline: often shaped by decision-makers.

Basic or Applied? You Decide

Situation A

A university study seeks a general explanation of why employees resist organisational change.

Basic Research

Situation B

A company investigates why its own employees are leaving and what corrective action it should take.

Applied Research
The distinction is mainly about the purpose of the research—not whether it is scientific.

How Can We Investigate?

QUANTITATIVE

Numbers & variables

Useful for measurement, relationships, comparisons and hypothesis testing.

Does supervisor support influence job satisfaction?

QUALITATIVE

Meaning & experience

Useful for deeper understanding through interviews, observations or text.

How do employees describe supervisor support?

MIXED METHODS

Both

Combine quantitative and qualitative evidence when useful.

Survey first, then interview employees to explain the patterns.

There is no universally “best” approach. The research problem and information needed help determine the appropriate approach.

Research Paradigms — Keep It Simple

A paradigm is a broad way of thinking about how knowledge about the world can be obtained.

POSITIVISM

MEASURE

Phenomena can be systematically observed, measured and classified.

CONSTRUCTIVISM

UNDERSTAND MEANING

People interpret and construct meaning from their experiences and social world.

PRAGMATISM

WHAT WORKS?

Use approaches that help answer the problem at hand.

Common associations: positivism ↔ quantitative; constructivism ↔ qualitative; pragmatism ↔ mixed methods. Useful associations—not rigid rules.
