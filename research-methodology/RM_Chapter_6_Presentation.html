Hypothesis testing: association and causation

The kind of question determines the evidence required.

Correlational

Is supervisor support related to turnover intention?

Causal

Does introducing a support programme reduce turnover intention?

An association alone cannot establish that one variable causes the other.

Programme group and comparison group

Question: does a supervisor-support programme reduce turnover intention?

Programme example: supervisor training, regular employee check-ins and help with work problems.

Programme group

Receives the new support programme.
Also called the intervention or treatment group.

Before: measure turnover intention
During: introduce the programme
After: measure turnover intention again

Comparison group

Continues with usual arrangements.
Shows what happens without the new programme.

Before: measure turnover intention
During: usual arrangements continue
After: measure turnover intention again
Compare changes in both groups over the same period. Keep groups as comparable as practical.

Extent of researcher interference

How much does the researcher manipulate and control the situation?

Minimal

Observe or measure existing conditions without manipulating the IV.

Moderate

Introduce a change in a natural setting.

Excessive / high

Use extensive manipulation and control in an artificial setting.

“Excessive” is the course term for high experimental control; it does not imply unethical conduct.

Strategies: experiment, survey and observation

Choose a strategy that can answer the question.

Experiment

Introduce a manipulation and compare outcomes. Useful for causal questions.

Survey

Collect standardised information from or about people to describe or examine relationships.

Observation

Systematically watch and record behaviour or events.

A survey can support different purposes. It is not automatically a descriptive study.

Mixed methods requires integration

Use qualitative and quantitative evidence together for a clear purpose.

Qualitative contribution

Interviews explore why employees feel unsupported.

Quantitative contribution

A survey examines how widely the issues occur and how variables relate.

Integrate the findings: use interview themes to inform the survey, or use interviews to interpret survey results.
Collecting two kinds of data without connecting them is insufficient as a rationale.

The respondent and unit of analysis may differ

Who provides the information is not always the entity being analysed.

Employee-level study

Employees report support and intention. Analyse differences between employees.

Restaurant-level study

Managers provide information about restaurants. Compare restaurant efficiency.

In the second example, the manager is an informant; the restaurant is the unit of analysis.

Time horizon

The question determines whether a snapshot is sufficient.

Cross-sectional

Measure the relevant constructs at one point or one collection period.

Longitudinal

Investigate constructs or change across multiple points in time.

Repeated cross-sectional studies use different samples. A panel follows the same units over time.

Revision: defend the design

Explain the choices rather than simply naming them.

Specify

What changes, where, who or what is analysed, and when?
