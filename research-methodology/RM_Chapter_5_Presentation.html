Concepts, constructs and variables

Move from the meaning of an idea to a defined representation for research.

Concept

A general idea used to describe something—for example, support.

Construct

An abstract concept deliberately defined for research—for example, perceived supervisor support.

Variable

A characteristic that can take different values across people or situations. It may be observed or latent.

Questionnaire responses can be observed indicators of an abstract construct.
Terms can overlap; define clearly how you use them in your study.

Theory & literature

Null and alternative hypotheses

Use the same variables and relationship in both statements.

H₀ · Null

There is no relationship between supervisor support and turnover intention.

H₁ · Alternative

There is a relationship between supervisor support and turnover intention.

These are introductory substantive statements. A statistical test requires a precise parameter-based formulation.
Findings may support a prediction; they do not prove the theory true.

Directional and non-directional statements

Direction should follow reasoning and prior evidence—not a guess.

Non-directional

Supervisor support is related to turnover intention.

Negative direction

Higher supervisor support is associated with lower turnover intention.

Positive direction

Higher supervisor support is associated with higher job satisfaction.

“If–then” is another wording format. Choose wording that states a specific, testable relationship.
Directional wording alone does not decide the full statistical testing procedure.
