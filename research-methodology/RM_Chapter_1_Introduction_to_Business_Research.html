1What is Research?

Research is a systematic process of collecting and analysing information to answer questions, understand problems and support decisions.

The key word is systematic. Research is more than searching online, asking a few friends, or making a decision based only on personal opinion. It follows an organised process and uses evidence.

Systematic

Follow an organised process.

Evidence

Collect relevant information or data.

Analysis

Examine evidence for patterns or explanations.

Decision

Use findings to answer questions or support action.
SYSTEMATIC→EVIDENCE→ANALYSIS→DECISION

3Research vs Everyday Decision-Making

Everyday managerial judgment

Experience • Intuition • Observation • Opinions • Assumptions

Useful, but sometimes insufficient for an important decision.

Research adds systematic evidence

Evidence can test whether the manager's initial explanation is actually supported.

See the salary assumption example
A manager believes employees are unhappy because they want higher salaries. Research might instead show that workload and poor supervisor support are stronger concerns.
Research does not replace managerial experience and judgment. It provides better evidence to support them.

4What is Business Research?

Business research is the systematic investigation of business-related questions, problems or opportunities to provide information that supports decision-making.

5From Business Problem to Managerial Decision

The central logic to remember. Detailed research methods come later in the course.

1
Business Problem

Identify a situation needing attention.

2
Question / Information Needed

What must management find out?

3
Collect Evidence

Gather relevant data systematically and ethically.

4
Analyse & Interpret

Identify patterns and explanations.

5
Managerial Decision

Use findings to support action.

Worked example: employee turnover
Problem: turnover increased → Question: why are employees leaving? → Evidence: surveys, interviews, HR records → Analysis: identify factors such as job satisfaction, workload and supervisor support → Decision: develop an evidence-based retention response.

6Why Business Research Matters to Managers

1. Identify problems

Determine what requires attention.

2. Understand causes

Investigate why it occurs.

3. Reduce uncertainty

Replace some assumptions with evidence.

4. Evaluate alternatives

Compare possible actions.

5. Make informed decisions

Combine evidence with managerial judgment.

6. Evaluate outcomes

Check whether an action worked.
Research supports managerial decision-making. It does not guarantee that every decision will be correct.

Cyntoq Corp. decision scenario
For serious misconduct involving senior management, an external researcher may be preferable where independence, confidentiality and perceived impartiality are important. For a routine operational problem, an internal researcher may be more appropriate when organisational knowledge, access, cost and implementation involvement matter more.

8Ethics in Business Research

Ethical research protects participants and preserves research integrity. Ethics applies throughout the research process.

Voluntary Participation & Informed Consent

Participants understand the research and are not improperly forced to participate.

Privacy, Anonymity & Confidentiality

Personal information and responses are appropriately protected.

Avoid Harm

Do not unnecessarily expose participants to physical, psychological, social or professional harm.

Honesty & Integrity

Do not fabricate data, manipulate results or deliberately misrepresent findings.
Ethics scenario: “voluntary” participation
A lecturer says participation is voluntary but students who refuse may lose marks. The threat to marks undermines genuinely voluntary participation.
Ethics scenario: “anonymous” employee survey
A survey is called anonymous but requires staff ID. The anonymity claim is questionable because responses can be linked to identifiable individuals.

✓Revision: What Must You Know?

You should be able to: define research; explain its purposes; explain business research and its value to managers; explain the problem-to-decision logic; compare internal and external researchers; justify a choice in a scenario; and identify basic ethical issues.

Q1. What is research, and why is systematic important?
Research is a systematic process of collecting and analysing information to answer questions, understand problems and support decisions. Systematic means the investigation follows an organised process rather than relying only on guesses or assumptions.
Q2. Explain TWO ways research assists managers.
Possible points: identify problems, understand causes, reduce uncertainty, evaluate alternatives, support informed decisions, or evaluate outcomes. Explain each selected point.
Q3. Give ONE advantage and ONE disadvantage of internal and external researchers.
Internal: strong organisational knowledge/easier access, but internal politics or fewer fresh ideas may be limitations. External: independence/broader experience/specialised expertise, but higher cost and time needed to understand the organisation.
Q4. Workplace bullying allegations involve senior managers. Which researcher may be appropriate, and why?
An external researcher may be appropriate because independence, confidentiality and perceived impartiality are particularly important in a sensitive case.
Q5. A survey is called anonymous but asks for employee ID. What ethical concern arises?
The anonymity claim is questionable because the employee ID can link responses to an identifiable participant. Privacy and confidentiality also require protection.
KEY TAKEAWAY
Business research is not simply about collecting data. It is a systematic and ethical process of using evidence to understand business problems and support better managerial decisions.
