The case: Campus Café

Campus Café operates three outlets near a university. During the current semester, its records show fewer repeat visits than during the previous semester. Customer comments also mention slow service, incorrect orders and unfriendly interactions.

The manager concludes:

“Students are not returning because our prices are too high. We should immediately reduce all prices.”

Before making this decision, the manager asks five regular customers who were available at one outlet whether they think the café is expensive. Four say yes. The manager treats this as confirmation that price is the main cause.

However, another staff member suggests investigating service quality and customer satisfaction before deciding what to change.

You have been asked to propose a study involving student customers of the three outlets during the next semester. The proposed outcome is revisit intention: customers’ intention to visit the café again.

Keep the concepts distinct: revisit intention and actual repeat visits are related but different.

Preliminary research summaries

Fictional teaching examples supplied for this activity. Do not cite these as real published studies.

Study A

A questionnaire survey of 300 student customers at university cafés found that higher perceived service quality was associated with higher customer satisfaction. Higher customer satisfaction was also associated with stronger revisit intention.

Study B

A questionnaire survey of 180 customers at shopping-mall cafés found no clear association between perceived service quality and revisit intention. Price perceptions were associated with revisit intention.

These summaries do not provide full information about sampling, measurement or analysis. Neither summary, by itself, establishes causation.
