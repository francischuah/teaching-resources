Stage 2 • In-Class Group Project

Analyse Your Group Sales Activity Dataset

Clean, summarise, compare, visualise, and interpret your own synthetic operational dataset.

Scenario

Your group has extracted 90 days of operational records from a sales activity system. The data include attendance, region assignment, daily promotional expenditure, and total sales for six sales managers. Your task is to prepare the data for analysis and answer a series of managerial questions.

Remember: Different groups will have different numerical results. Your marks depend on correct analysis, appropriate output, and sound interpretation—not on matching another group's numbers.

Part A — Data preparation and cleaning

  1. Inspect the dataset for blank values in Daily_Promotion_Spend and Total_Sales.
  2. Determine whether these blanks are errors or logically missing values associated with Status = Absent.
  3. Explain why blank values should not automatically be replaced with zero.
  4. Prepare an analysis-ready view for sales analysis using only appropriate records.

Part B — Attendance analysis

  1. Create a PivotTable showing the number of Present and Absent records for each manager.
  2. Calculate each manager's attendance rate and absenteeism rate.
  3. Identify the manager with the highest absenteeism rate.
  4. Comment on whether absenteeism is concentrated among particular managers or relatively evenly distributed.
  5. Compare absenteeism across the three months. Is it increasing, decreasing, or relatively stable?
  6. Create a Region × Status crosstab or PivotTable. Does absenteeism appear more common in any region?

Part C — Sales performance analysis

  1. Calculate total sales for each manager.
  2. Calculate average daily sales for each manager using valid active-day records.
  3. Identify the manager with the highest total sales.
  4. Identify the manager with the highest average daily sales.
  5. Explain why the manager with the highest total sales may not necessarily have the highest average daily sales.
  6. Compare total and average sales across the four regions.

Part D — Time-based analysis

  1. Group the Date variable by month.
  2. Create a PivotTable showing total sales by month.
  3. Create an appropriate chart to show the three-month sales trend.
  4. Repeat the analysis for total promotional expenditure by month.
  5. Comment on whether sales and promotional expenditure appear to move together over time.

Part E — Promotional expenditure and efficiency

  1. Compare total promotional expenditure by manager.
  2. Compare average daily promotional expenditure by manager.
  3. Identify the manager who spends the most and the manager who spends the least.
  4. Calculate a simple sales-efficiency measure:
Sales Efficiency = Total Sales / Total Promotion Spend
  1. Compare sales efficiency across managers.
  2. Does the manager who spends the most also generate the highest sales efficiency? Explain.

Part F — Relationship analysis

  1. Using valid Present records only, create a scatter plot with Daily_Promotion_Spend on the X-axis and Total_Sales on the Y-axis.
  2. Describe the direction of the relationship.
  3. Comment on whether the relationship appears weak, moderate, or strong based on the visual pattern.
  4. Add a linear trendline.
  5. Display the R² value and explain what it tells you about the relationship between promotional expenditure and sales.
  6. Select one manager and repeat the scatter-plot analysis. Compare the manager-specific pattern with the overall pattern.

Part G — Managerial interpretation

Prepare a short group conclusion answering the following:

  1. Which manager appears to perform best overall?
  2. Which manager may require managerial attention?
  3. Is absenteeism an important concern in your dataset?
  4. Which region performs best?
  5. Does higher promotional expenditure appear to be associated with higher sales?
  6. What is one practical recommendation you would make to management based on your findings?
Support every conclusion with evidence from your PivotTables, calculations, or charts. Avoid making claims that are not supported by your own dataset.

Suggested outputs

Your group should be prepared to show:

  • At least one cleaned analysis view
  • Attendance PivotTable
  • Manager sales PivotTable
  • Regional sales PivotTable
  • Monthly trend chart
  • Promotion expenditure summary
  • Sales-efficiency calculation
  • Scatter plot with trendline and R²
  • Short managerial conclusion
